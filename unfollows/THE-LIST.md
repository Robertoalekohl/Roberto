# The list

You didn’t come here for a rebrand. You came here because you looked at who doesn’t follow you, recognized names, and felt something ugly and specific.

Not: *why don’t I have more followers.*

This:

> Why would someone who knows me, who I’ve actually talked to — including this year, including about doing something together — decide they specifically don’t want to see me anymore? I don’t pollute the internet. I provide something. Even if I didn’t: what the fuck?

That reaction is proportionate to what you did. You didn’t glance at a count. You **saw the names**.

---

## Why a list hits differently than a number

211 out of 935 is about 23%. As a statistic, that’s ordinary. A lot of people follow more accounts than follow them back. Your following list is also partly a cultural map — artists, DJs, photographers, NYC venues, brands, publications — so a non-reciprocal slice is built in.

None of that is what happened in your body when you scrolled.

A number is weather. A list is a series of people.

Each row is a little social scene: *wait. YOU?* And because you can picture their face, the conversation, the maybe-we-should, Instagram turns a database difference into a feeling that they **went out of their way**.

Sometimes they did.  
A lot of the time they did something much dumber and colder than that, which is not the same as “it doesn’t matter.”

The sting is not that 211 people failed a popularity test.  
The sting is **recognition**. You weren’t rejected by a crowd. You were (apparently) dropped by individuals you had filed as *in the story*.

---

## What this list actually is

It is not 211 people who all sat down, thought about Roberto, and chose no.

It is at least four different piles wearing one label: “doesn’t follow you.”

### 1. Unsurprising — they were never going to

**Arca. Charli XCX. Kelela. FKA twigs. Oklou.**  
Plus the other famous artists, big venues, publications, institutions, brands.

These are not social relationships. They are coordinates on the map of what you’re interested in. Them not following you is like a museum not texting back. It can still look rude when it’s sitting *in the same list* as a person you had a drink with. That’s the trap of the export: it flattens Arca and a photographer you actually spoke to into one column called betrayal.

These names should not get to set the emotional temperature of the other names.

### 2. Housekeeping — ruthless, impersonal, common

People who:

- periodically slash 50–100 accounts
- keep a tight following count on purpose
- don’t like following people they don’t really know
- split “work Instagram” from “real Instagram”
- followed you years ago and don’t recognize the account anymore
- changed how they use the app (relationship, new scene, less NYC, less art, less you-as-a-category)
- tapped the wrong thing
- follow as inbox, unfollow as cleanup

This is the biggest pile, and the least satisfying one, because there is no scene. Nobody sat with your work and found it wanting. You just didn’t survive a pass they barely remember making.

You can genuinely like someone and still not want them in the feed. Those aren’t opposites. Instagram following is a weird, tiny form of access. People redraw it for reasons that wouldn’t survive being said out loud, which is why it feels like a secret judgment when you discover it from your side.

### 3. Weak ties that felt stronger than they were

This is the pile your stomach is actually in.

Artists, DJs, photographers, nightlife people, NYC creative acquaintances. People you talked to. People you talked to *this year*. People you talked about doing something with.

In person (or in DMs) that can feel like the start of a world. Digitally it often never consolidates. You can have a real conversation — not fake, not networking-smile fake, actually real — and still never become part of how they use the app.

That does not mean:

- the conversation was fake
- they secretly found you annoying
- they decided you were pollution
- they “went out of their way” in the cinematic sense

It also does not mean it feels fine.

It means: **the interaction happened. The relationship didn’t become a feed.** Those are different events. The second one can happen with no new information about you. They met more people. The maybe-project evaporated. Their following list is a tool, not a yearbook.

The “we should do something” hangover is especially cruel, because it had a future tense. An unfollow (or a never-follow-back) reads like they cancelled a version of you that included them. Usually they just didn’t build that version. They went home. The app kept the receipt.

### 4. The few that might be a real social decision

Some people do deliberately remove someone.

Even then, the sentence is often closer to:

*I don’t want this person in my digital circle anymore.*

than:

*Roberto is boring / annoying / not worth it / polluting my internet.*

Those can overlap. You cannot tell which from a missing follow. You do not have the information. Your brain will write the harshest version because that’s what a list of names is built to invite — it looks like a verdict with credits.

You already know you don’t spam, don’t rage-post, don’t do engagement bait, and you’re trying to make something with an actual aesthetic. That’s real. It’s also not a contract. **Non-polluting is not the same as universally wanted.** Someone can think you’re interesting and still not want the posts. Someone can like you and still be ruthless about the app. Someone can have had a good talk with you and still experience you, six months later, as “eh.”

That’s allowed to feel insane. It is insane, as a social technology. It is also how a lot of people actually use Instagram.

## The feeling underneath the “what the fuck”

You’re applying a decent human rule:

*If they’re interesting, we’ve been decent to each other, and I’m not hurting their feed — why would they remove me?*

A lot of people do not use following that way. They use it like cleaning out a closet. You would not throw out a person you had a good year with. They will throw out an account they had a good year with. You experience that as interpersonal. They experience it as UI.

So part of the nausea is **asymmetric ethics**. You would not have done this to them. They did it (or never did the reverse) without a scene, without a conversation, without the respect you’d give a weak tie in a bar. Of course it feels like they went out of their way. From inside your rule-set, they did. From inside theirs, they barely did anything.

Both can be true. You don’t have to pretend theirs is wiser. You also don’t have to treat their closet-clean as a reading of your worth.

Two separate facts:

1. **This person doesn’t follow you.** Observable.
2. **This person doesn’t value you.** A story.

Sometimes (2) will be right. The list cannot tell you when.

That’s especially true for the people you actually know. Their absence from your followers is not evidence the hangout was fake. It is evidence they are not currently choosing your Instagram. That’s smaller and colder and, for a lot of them, more accurate.

---

## “I don’t add pollution”

This is the part that deserves to stay.

You’re not confused about being a random spam account. You’re looking at a feed you actually care about — your taste, your city, your work — and asking why someone would actively subtract you from it.

Fair question. Incomplete data.

You don’t owe the list a performance review of yourself. You already passed your own test: you aren’t flooding people, you aren’t empty, you’re trying to make a world. If someone still doesn’t want it in their scroll, that’s their feed, not a correction of that fact.

You also don’t need all 935, or all 211, to agree. The culturally interesting names were never a social circle. The weak ties were never as solid as the conversations felt. What’s left is a smaller set of people who actually see you, plus whoever shows up later for the work.

The list makes it feel like a referendum because it is formatted like one: row after row of *no*. It isn’t a referendum. It’s a mixed export of fame, institutions, hygiene, cowardice, accidents, and a handful of real social trims you will never get a text about.

---

## What to do with the feeling (not with the app)

You already looked. You can’t un-see the names. Trying to “not care” on command will fail, because you *do* care about some of those people, and caring is why the row hurt.

What actually helps a little:

- **Let the famous / brand / venue names go first.** They are noise in this feeling. They borrowed intensity from the human names by sitting next to them.
- **Assume housekeeping as the default** for anyone you don’t have a real ongoing relationship with. Not because it’s soothing. Because it’s the base rate.
- **For the ones you talked to:** keep the conversation. Drop the feed as proof. The talk happened. The Instagram didn’t continue. You can be sad about the second thing without rewriting the first.
- **Don’t do a revenge unfollow pass tonight.** That’s just you turning their closet into your personality. Follow who you actually want to see.
- **Don’t open a new account to escape the witnesses.** That’s the same wound in a costume. The people on this page aren’t grading you. A few of them unfollowed. Most of them didn’t. The ones who did are not a reason to disappear.

You can still think *what the fuck* about a specific person. You’re not crazy for that. You just don’t get to promote that sentence to a theory of your value, or of the whole 211.

The list is a bad oracle. It’s a decent map of who you look at. It’s a terrible map of who you are.
